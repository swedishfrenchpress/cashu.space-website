## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely excellent site with one architectural risk and one strategic contradiction.

The craft is not in question. The doctrine is real rather than asserted — one hairline per page, no shadow outside the sanctioned navbar box, three typefaces doing three jobs, and figures that carry the argument instead of decorating it. The previous round's dynamic problems are fixed, and the detector agrees.

What's left is sharper and more interesting than "polish this." First, **the entire page is gated behind React hydration**, so the no-JS path renders beautifully while the slow-JS and failed-JS paths render nothing. Second, and bigger: **the composition says manifesto and the metrics say funnel.** PRODUCT.md ranks "installed a wallet" first, but the wallet ask appears exactly once, in the hero, and the page's most-remembered moment — the Twilight Stack close — offers only READ THE SPEC. The single biggest opportunity is to make the ending serve the thing you said you optimize for, without giving up an inch of the register.

## What's Working

1. **The figures persuade, and they're addressed to an expert.** Fig. 01's binary insight, the mint/melt crossing arrows that carry the protocol's own verbs, real NUT-23 request/response bodies. This audience trusts artifacts over claims, and the site spends its budget on artifacts. The `Amount` component's single scale jump — Pixel at 1.5rem against 0.75rem mono annotations — gives every plate one unambiguous entry point.
2. **The disclaimer is the best-designed element on the site.** "Mints are operated by independent parties; balances held with a mint are a claim on that mint, not a deposit. Read the spec before trusting anyone, including us." Letting it span the full footer grid rather than the 75ch measure makes it read as an instrument, not fine print. Arguing against your own authority buys more trust from this audience than any badge could.
3. **The no-JS fallback exists and works.** `:where(html.js) .reveal { opacity: 0 }` (`globals.css:424`) means that with scripting off, the site renders fully static. Very few teams actually build this. It is also, ironically, the mechanism behind the P0 below.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Every visible element is gated behind hydration, so a slow or failed JS load shows a blank page.**
- **What:** `layout.tsx:106–111` stamps `html.js` from a parser-blocking script. That immediately activates `:where(html.js) .reveal { opacity: 0 }` (`globals.css:424`). From that instant until React mounts and each `Reveal`'s effect fires, the whole page is invisible — navbar included, since `SiteHeader` wraps its row in `<Reveal immediate>` (`site-header.tsx:150`). Verified in source. **Evidence caveat, stated plainly:** Assessment A sat on a blank page for 10+ seconds with zero console errors, but that was a dev server that had stopped hydrating, so it is not production-representative — Assessment B loaded all three routes fine in the same window. The architecture is confirmed; the production frequency is unmeasured.
- **Why it matters:** You correctly decided a spec that only renders with scripting enabled would be a broken promise, and built for JS-off. The uncovered case is the *more common* one: JS on, chunk slow or failed. That path is strictly worse than the one you defended against, because it never resolves. This is the canonical document for a protocol, read on phones over hostile networks.
- **Fix:** Invert the gate. Keep content visible by default and have the client add a `reveal-armed` class only after React mounts, so an un-hydrated page is a static page rather than an empty one. Cheaper stopgap: a `setTimeout`/`requestIdleCallback` kill-switch that strips `html.js` after ~1.5s.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] "Six implementations, one spec." sits directly above a card that adds to eight.**
- **What:** Verified in source. `REPOS` has 8 entries. `IMPLEMENTATIONS` (`reference-implementations.tsx:223–225`) filters out `nuts` (Spec) and `awesome-cashu` (Resources) → **6**. But `REMAINING` (`:218`) is `REPOS.length - FEATURED.length` = 8 − 3 = **5**, rendered at `:263` as "+ 5 more in the cashubtc org" beneath 3 listed rows. The reader computes 3 + 5 = 8; the headline says Six. The comment at `:220` claims the two "can never disagree" — they already do, because they use different denominators.
- **Why it matters:** This is the one section whose entire argument is *we count things honestly*, on a site whose disclaimer says "Read the spec before trusting anyone, including us." Your audience audits details; this is the detail they will audit.
- **Fix:** `const REMAINING = IMPLEMENTATIONS - FEATURED.length;` and reword to "+ 3 more implementations in the cashubtc org".
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] The second of the two sanctioned CTAs has a 1.27:1 boundary.**
- **What:** `.btn-secondary` (`globals.css:601`) is `background: var(--card)` on `--paper` with a `--hair` `#e4e4e7` border — **1.27:1** against the page (I recomputed this independently; it checks out). `.btn-secondary--on-ink` (`globals.css:646`) is worse: fill 1.19:1, border **2.01:1** against black. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for a control's identifying boundary.
- **Why it matters:** "READ THE SPEC" is one of only two jobs the Two-CTA Rule recognises, and in the hero it currently reads as a line of text with a ghost around it. The doctrine — flat, shadowless, hairline — is actively suppressing the affordance it depends on. Note the detector's clean contrast result does not cover this; it tests text, not control boundaries.
- **Fix:** Step the secondary's rest-state border to `--muted` (`#71717a`, 4.8:1) rather than only on hover; do the same on-ink. No shadow required, doctrine fully intact.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] The homepage never asks for its primary conversion again after the hero.**
- **What:** "Get a wallet" appears once, at `page.tsx:36`. Across ~7,800px of subsequent scroll the only wallet route is a `.btn-secondary` "EXPLORE WALLETS" inside pillar 01. The closing CTA (`site-footer.tsx:65–70`) offers only "Read the spec".
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md's success order is (a) installed a wallet, (b) bookmarked the spec. Peak-end says the close is remembered disproportionately, and this one closes on documentation. The composition and the stated metric disagree, and the footer is where they disagree.
- **Fix:** Make the Twilight Stack sign-off a paired row — "Get a wallet" as `.btn-primary--on-ink` beside "Read the spec" as `.btn-secondary--on-ink`. That is precisely the Two-CTA Rule applied at the page's peak instead of only at its opening. No new component, no new colour.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] All four feature blocks put the figure above its own headline.**
- **What:** `tabbed-feature.tsx:171` renders `<ProtocolDemo>` before `feature-block__text` at `:173`. Desktop reading order is: unexplained technical plate → `Fig. NN` caption → FIGURE/CODE toggle → *then* "Hold ecash on your phone."
- **Why it matters:** A spec captions figures *because* the prose introduced them. Here the plate arrives cold and the reader must hold `keyset 00ffd48b8f5ecf80` in working memory until the thesis shows up ~700px later. For the target persona this is the peak moment; for anyone adjacent it's a wall. It also pushes each block's CTA far below its first pixel.
- **Fix:** Move `feature-block__text` above `<ProtocolDemo>`. The plate then reads as evidence for a claim already made — which is the RFC register the North Star is after.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] `/wallets` routes 13 destinations with thin decision support and doubles every tab stop.**
- **What:** `wallets/page.tsx:33–74` — Athenut carries no `facts` at all; Cashu.me carries one ("PWA"). Each row renders the wordmark *and* an OPEN button pointing at the identical href (`:139`, `:164`): 26 tab stops for 13 destinations, and a screen-reader user hears every target twice. The H1 is "Wallets." but 5 of 13 rows are libraries and mint tooling.
- **Why it matters:** This page exists to serve success-metric (a) and it is the highest-anxiety decision on the site. "Not an endorsement" is honest; "no basis for choosing" is a different problem wearing the same principle.
- **Fix:** (i) Drop the duplicate — make the whole row the link, or keep OPEN and demote the wordmark to plain text. (ii) Give every entry at least one verifiable fact in the existing mono register (custody model, platform, last release). (iii) Retitle to "Wallets and tooling." or move Libraries/Tools to their own route.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Questions to Consider

1. **If the whole page is invisible until React mounts, in what sense is this primary-source material?** A published RFC is legible on a bad connection, in a text browser, in an archive. Your `:where(html.js)` fallback proves you already believe that. So why does the *default* path — JS on, network slow — produce a blank sheet?
2. **`/wallets` refuses to differentiate on principle. Is that honesty, or abdication?** "Not an endorsement" protects neutrality, but the visitor still has to choose one, and they will now choose on wordmark aesthetics or alphabetical position — a strictly worse signal than "iOS, self-custodial, last release March 2026". Stating verifiable facts is not endorsing. What are you protecting by withholding them?
3. **The site's ending is "Read the spec." Is that what you want remembered?** You have built a page whose disclaimer argues against trusting you, whose figures assume expertise, and whose final gesture points at documentation. That is a coherent identity — a manifesto. But PRODUCT.md's #1 metric is a wallet install. Which is the site: the manifesto or the funnel?
4. **Is the "Properties of the protocol" bento still earning its 1,200px?** Four generic line-art cards sit between the four spec plates and the eight-repo directory — the two most specific things you have. It is the only section that could ship unchanged on a competitor's site. If the figures carry the argument, what is this section adding beyond a familiar shape?
