## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored in language, borrowed in form — and the borrowed forms carry the middle of the page.**

This is not category-interchangeable. The copy could not be lifted onto another product: *"nut:404 / Not specified."*, *"Bitcoin in, bitcoin out."*, *"a claim on that mint, not a deposit"*, *"Read the spec before trusting anyone, including us."* The anti-references are genuinely avoided — zero gradients, zero shields, zero coins, zero badge chips — and the honesty apparatus is real code rather than a pose: the "Six" in the implementations headline and the "+ 3 more" in the Card share one derived denominator, `targetOf()` prints the real destination host under every directory wordmark, and the footer's AI-links block carries its own recorded failure mode in a comment instead of shipping quietly.

But look at where the forms come from. The homepage's middle is a 40/60 sticky split taken from a reference layout. The masthead is taken from a layout reference. The press band is modeled on the Griffin stories layout. The four figures are `thinking-orbs`, a library built to show an AI assistant thinking. The one form the site drew itself — the ASCII field carrying the blind-signature round trip — is deleted, and DESIGN.md's own Honest-Network Rule calls it "the scene that earns the motion." What remains drawn here rather than sourced: the Spec pane, the corner registration marks, the cipher pass, the hairline draw. Those four are excellent. They are also small.

**Structural sameness.** Three of four homepage bands are the same shape: left-aligned headline, lead paragraph, wide object, air. At 1440 the split sheet is 57% of the page and it is one row repeated four times.

**The clearest missed opportunity.** Geist Pixel Square — a whole third face, loaded, and described in DESIGN.md §5 as "the only typographic flourish in the system" — renders in exactly five places sitewide, three inside the CBOR listing and two inside a keyboard overlay most visitors never open. Meanwhile the two most recognisable artefacts Cashu owns, a real `cashuB…` token string and NUT numbers, appear nowhere in the site's own voice. The 404 already proves the device works (`nut:404`).

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json src/app src/components` over 24 files: **exit 0, zero findings.** A control run with `--no-config` returns exactly 2 (`broken-image` on `in-the-press.tsx:186` and `site-footer.tsx:144`), confirming the clean run is genuine suppression of two pre-verified entries and nothing else. The in-page detector on the live pages reported 13–16 on `/`, 1 on `/wallets`, 0 on the 404 — **every one resolved to a false positive or a pre-verified ignore entry**:

- `wide-tracking` ×12 on the protocol-parts property items — not body text. These are Geist Mono 0.75rem at 0.06em, the Micro/Label role in DESIGN.md §3, the same tracking §5 mandates for uppercase button copy.
- `cramped-padding` on `.site-nav-panel` — measured the *closed* disclosure panel (`390×0`, `opacity: 0`, `grid-template-rows: 0fr`). Padding is deliberately state-scoped.
- `line-length` on `.footer-disclaimer` and `heading-rhythm` ×3 on `.press-card__title` — both already in `ignore.md`.
- A contrast reading of 1.0 on the current nav link — an artifact of the masthead rendering each label twice for the wipe. Composited ratio at that pixel is 17.72.

Heading order is clean on all three routes (one `h1`, no skipped levels). Zero horizontal overflow at 360/390/768/1024/1440/1920/2560. Zero images broken at runtime. Every one of 25/27/4 tab stops carries a visible focus ring in both schemes, including the three per-ground overrides. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` everything suppresses correctly except a residual 150ms button colour transition. The **Set-Once Rule is verified empirically**: the hero region is pixel-identical at t=2.5s, 5.5s and 9.0s, with zero DOM mutations over a six-second observation at rest.

**The detector is clean and the design is not.** Every issue below was found by reading and measuring, not by a rule.

**Visual overlays.** Both assessments ran fully headless (deliberately — Dark Reader on the primary browser corrupts computed styles and screenshots). **No user-visible overlay exists in your browser.** All evidence above is console-channel and composited-pixel measurement.

## Overall Impression

The craft floor here is genuinely high, and higher than the score suggests. The arrival sequence is the best thing on the site: a hairline drawing across the fold, one word resolving out of hex, and then a full stop that holds for the length of the visit. It reads as engineered rather than animated, which is exactly the emotion the brand wants. The keyboard and focus work is better than most shipped design systems. The honesty apparatus is enforced in code.

The single biggest opportunity is **dark mode**, and it is not a polish item. Both of the homepage's signature compositions are value-contrast compositions, and at night neither has any value contrast. The 40/60 split collapses to one flat black field. The bright-plate-over-dark-listing collapses to a white card floating next to unbounded code. The masthead's brand plate — "the one place the bar breaks its ink" — becomes invisible, taking route feedback and the site's most elaborated interaction with it. On 2026-08-16 the always-dark implementations band was rejected because a section stuck in dark mode read as a bug. The inverse failure is shipping right now, it is harder to spot, and it is the reason this scores 27 instead of 32.

## What's Working

**The arrival, and its discipline under `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Verified under `reduce`: `hero-rule-draw` returns an empty animation list, the cipher overlay is never created, all four canvases render identical frames 1.5s apart, `.reveal` drops to `0s` with `transform: none`. This works because the discipline is structural — `orb-figure.tsx` gates its rAF loop on IntersectionObserver *and* `document.visibilityState` *and* a live-tracked media query. The Set-Once Rule is not an aspiration in a doc; it is measurable at the pixel.

**The honesty apparatus is code, not copy.** The implementations count and the "+ 3 more" share one denominator, so the section whose entire argument is honest counting cannot fail its own arithmetic. The directory prints the real host under every wordmark. This is why the site reads as primary-source material: the claims and the code agree.

**Focus and keyboard craft.** 25 tab stops on the homepage, every one scrolled into view, every one ringed, and the three exceptions are all correct: `-3px` inset on the corner-anchored brand plate, a literal `#ffffff` on ink grounds, a literal `#18181b` inside the always-light Card. The cipher pass fires on pointer only and never on focus, specifically so a keyboard visitor is never left reading a blanked CTA. That reasoning is written down and it is actually implemented.

**The 404 is the only place the site is funny, and it is funny in register.** `nut:404` / "Not specified." / "This path does not appear in the protocol." That is authored character, not a template.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Both signature compositions collapse in dark mode

**What.** `--panel` is `#000000` in `:root` and overridden to `#131316` in both dark blocks (globals.css:216, 243) against `--paper` `#0a0a0b`: **1.07:1**. The Spec pane's `#18181b` against the same page: **1.12:1**. In light these are 21:1 and 16:1 compositions; at night they are flat fields.

**Why it matters.** This is the homepage's entire middle plus its closing argument, dead for every dark-scheme visitor. The 40/60 split becomes one black field with a faint 1px line at 40%. The "bright plate over a dark listing" becomes a white card next to unbounded code. This is the same class of defect as the always-dark implementations band that was rejected on 2026-08-16, inverted so it only shows at night.

**Fix.** Three coordinated changes, all inside the grey ramp: set `--panel: #000000` in both dark blocks; give `.protocol-parts__list` a `border-left: 1px solid #3f3f46` in dark so the split has an edge even when the fields nearly match; give the Spec pane wrapper a `1px solid var(--hair)` rim — the precedent already exists on the Card three lines away, for the same reason in the opposite scheme.

*Also worth correcting while in there:* the comment at globals.css:51–54 says the panel ramp is "deliberately NOT repeated in the two dark blocks" because these are "values on an ink ground that never flips." The ramp values are white alphas and do survive both grounds, so nothing is broken — but the premise is false, `--panel` flips 165 lines below, and that false premise is plausibly how the override went unnoticed.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P0] The masthead plate is invisible in dark, taking route feedback and the hover wipe with it

**What.** `--nav-plate: var(--paper)` (globals.css:101). In dark that resolves to `#0a0a0b` against `--nav-bg` `#18181b`: **1.14:1**, verified. The plate is DESIGN.md §5's "one place the bar breaks its ink," and it has one other consumer — the `clip-path` wipe that also marks the current route. Both are, at night, an invisible rectangle. The most elaborated interaction on the site (label rendered twice, 460ms, edge passing through the letterforms) delivers a text-brightness bump from 66% to 96% white and nothing else. `.site-nav-panel__cta` takes the same token, so the mobile panel's closing slab has the identical problem.

**Why it matters.** Route feedback is a status signal, not a flourish. A dark-scheme visitor on `/wallets` gets no indication in the bar that they are on `/wallets`.

**Fix.** Do not put a literal back on the plate — that version was rejected on sight and "the page punched through the bar" is the right concept. Move the other value instead: raise `--nav-bg` in dark to `#3f3f46`. `#0a0a0b` on `#3f3f46` is **3.0:1**, the plate stays exactly "the page," one token keeps two consumers, and the bar still steps clearly off the `#0a0a0b` page. Fixes the mobile panel slab for free.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] The press band is an undocumented third always-dark section, on a justification that died the same day

**What.** `in-the-press.tsx:133` hardcodes `bg-black text-white`. The fixed-value list at globals.css:12–25 names four always-dark surfaces and does not include it. The band's own docstring (`in-the-press.tsx:11`) reads *"Sits on Ink (like the reference-implementations section it follows)"* — and that section stopped sitting on Ink on 2026-08-16, when this exact treatment was rejected there. The comment describes a page that no longer exists. Its `border-top: 1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.08)` was drawn to separate two black sections and now does nothing against the Paper section above it.

**Why it matters.** In light mode the page now runs white document → hard black band → black footer. It is the same slab-stuck-in-dark-mode that was already rejected once, 200 lines of scroll higher, on the same day.

**Fix.** Decide which it is. Either bring the band onto `--paper`/`--ink` with `grayscale(1)` imagery intact and the wordmarks flipped by token rather than by `brightness(0) invert(1)`; or, if it is genuinely the top of the closing Twilight stack, say so — add it to the globals.css:12–25 list and to DESIGN.md §5, and replace the stale docstring. Right now it is drift with a dead reason attached.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] The homepage has no `<main>`, and the 404 has no chrome at all

**What.** Measured landmarks: `/` → `main: 0`; `/wallets` → `main: 1`; 404 → `main: 1, nav: 0, footer: 0`. `layout.tsx:154` renders `{children}` bare and `page.tsx:12` opens with a `<div>`, so the skip link on the site's primary page lands on a `<section>` inside no main region. Separately, `not-found.tsx` renders neither `SiteHeader` nor `SiteFooter`, directly contradicting the comment at `wallets/page.tsx:214` ("the twilight stack closes every page … not into a dead end").

**Why it matters.** Landmark navigation is how a screen-reader user skips the masthead on the page they will spend the most time on. And the 404 is the highest-abandon surface on the site: a stale `/mints` or `/docs` link drops the visitor on a page with two links, no nav, no wallets, no spec CTA, and no disclaimer.

**Fix.** Wrap the homepage sections in `<main id="main-content">` and move the id off `.hero-spec`. Add `<SiteHeader />` and `<SiteFooter />` to `not-found.tsx` and delete its bespoke brand row. While there, add `tabindex="-1"` to the skip-link target so AT focus actually moves.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The hero headline breaks in three lines at every width, and DESIGN.md says it breaks in two

**What.** Measured line counts: 768→3, 1024→3, 1280→3, 1440→3, 1920→3, 2560→3, 360→5. DESIGN.md §3 states: *"The headline now breaks in two at every width from 768px up."* That is false at every width tested. The headline's escape from the page shell (`width: min(calc(100vw - 2*var(--page-x)), 1440px)`) was taken *specifically* to buy the two-line break — the CSS comment computes "~1283px to break cleanly in two" — so the one element on the site that leaves the page shell is paying an alignment cost for a benefit that never arrived. The rag also hangs the preposition: `OPEN SOURCE / ECASH FOR / BITCOIN.` at desktop.

**Why it matters.** This is the page peak and the site's single strongest move, and its shape is currently an accident of `text-wrap: balance` rather than a composition — which DESIGN.md §3 explicitly forbids ("The line break in Display copy is part of the composition, not an accident of viewport").

**Fix.** Take DESIGN.md's own instruction: *"if the hero copy grows too long for Display, shorten the copy."* Either set the break explicitly (`Open source ecash` / `for bitcoin.` as two block spans, which delivers the documented two-line break and kills the hanging FOR at every width), or shorten to "Ecash for bitcoin." and let the deck carry "open source." Then re-derive or retire the shell escape, since its stated justification is gone.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

## Questions to Consider

**1. The Set-Once Rule stops at the fold. Why?** The hero's `listening` orb was deleted on the argument that "an ambient loop answers nothing" and that "confident through silence is contradicted by a hero that will not stop moving." Four ambient loops from the same library, through the same engine, at comparable scale, still run permanently 800px lower. The Honest-Network Rule was amended to admit them as "material" — but that amendment answers the *honesty* objection, not the *screensaver* one. If an ambient loop answers nothing at 900px, what does it answer at 1,700px?

**2. What if there is one figure, and the reader drives it?** The blind-signature round trip is recoverable at `git show 5e4c0e8^:src/lib/ascii/bdhke.ts`, and the Set-Once Rule explicitly leaves the door open: "if one returns it should advance on scroll or on arrival, so the reader causes the motion." The split sheet is already four scroll-linked entries. What if it were four entries and **one** figure, advancing one stage per entry — minted at 01, blind-signed at 02, specified at 03, travelling at 04? That is the only move on the table that satisfies the Honest-Network Rule's *first* branch and the Set-Once Rule at the same time.

**3. If mint trust is a real visitor job, where is the mints page — and if it isn't, why is it in PRODUCT.md?** The registry pattern already exists and already works: name them, state verifiable facts, endorse nothing. What would a `/mints` page look like that is a register rather than a recommendation, and would "including us" finally have somewhere load-bearing to sit?

**4. The masthead runs a wall clock. What would it read if it read something the protocol knows?** `HH:MM UTC` is the one element in the bar that could appear unchanged on any developer-tools site. The slot is 9ch, mono, minute-granular, already built. What if it carried the current NUT count, or the spec version — a number true about *this protocol* rather than about the visitor's timezone?
