## Design Context

This project has a documented design system. Before generating UI, styling components, or making visual choices, read:

- **[PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md)** — strategic spec: register (`brand`), users, brand personality, anti-references, design principles.
- **[DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md)** — visual spec: tokens, type scale, components, named rules, do's and don'ts. Follows the [Stitch DESIGN.md format](https://stitch.withgoogle.com/docs/design-md/format/).
- **[.impeccable/design.json](.impeccable/design.json)** — machine-readable sidecar with tonal ramps, motion, breakpoints, and component HTML/CSS snippets.

**North Star:** *"The Open Specification"* — cashu.space dresses an open protocol in the visual register of a published RFC. Monochrome by doctrine, GT-Standard carries the page, Geist Pixel Square reserved for protocol notation (amounts, mint ids, version strings).

**Hard rules (see DESIGN.md for the full set):**

- **The site is light only** (the One-Scheme Rule, DESIGN.md §2, user-directed 2026-08-17). No dark mode, no `prefers-color-scheme` branch, no `data-theme` override, no toggle, no saved preference; `color-scheme: light` on `:root` keeps the browser's own controls light too. **That is not the same as having no dark surfaces** — the Twilight Stack, the protocol-parts column, the masthead and the Spec pane are still dark plates, and the implementations Card is still a light one. They are *fixed-value surfaces*, dark by composition on a light page. Don't re-add a scheme branch to any of them: each used to carry a second dark value only because a value picked against `#ffffff` contrasts with nothing at `#0a0a0b`, and with one ground there is one value. Where a token still indirects (`--nav-plate` → `--paper`) that is deliberate — the plate is the page punched through the bar, and a literal would keep the pixel and lose the reason.
- Greys shift by lightness, never by hue. Colour exists in exactly two places, both named in DESIGN.md §2: third-party marks (Depicted-World Exception) and the signal green (Signal-Green Exception, user-directed 2026-08-16). The green has **exactly one licensed consumer** as of 2026-08-18: the 7px square opening the wallet-directory status tag. It is never type, a fill, a glow, a gradient, a hover, a focus ring, or a success/error state. **One consumer, so one token** — `--signal-page` (`#1b9d55`). The second, `--signal` (`#6fe3a4`, the mint picked against the always-dark protocol-parts column), was deleted with the property lists that used it rather than left in `:root` unused, which is how a bounded exception widens by accident. The `-page` suffix stays because the two were never interchangeable: `#6fe3a4` measures 1.59:1 on Paper and all but vanishes there, so a future fixed-dark consumer needs a value measured against *that* ground, not the deleted mint reinstated. **Scale is the whole exception**: its largest instance anywhere is 7 pixels square. It was briefly given the hero's pointer plume on 2026-08-16 and taken back the same day — at hero scale a borrowed mint accent read as the pastel/Web3 register PRODUCT.md rules out, and `--signal` itself came from aspensearch.com. - **A route change is a wipe, not a cut; the footer is uncovered, not faded; the press rail can be thrown** (the Curtain, Uncovered-Footer and Thrown-Rail Rules, DESIGN.md §4, all user-directed 2026-08-21). `motion-plus` is installed as `"motion-plus": "npm:@motionplus/core@^2.12.0"` — note **Motion+ ships through two channels and only one installs a package**: `components.json`/`shadcn add @motion/*` copies source into the repo and installs nothing, while `.npmrc`'s `@motionplus` scope is the actual package. Also: falloffs multiply **occupancy, not alpha** (fading type with alpha greys the letterforms into a smudge — the ASCII field's lesson); the mask fits the **union of `.hero-spec__content`'s children**, not the block, which would clear the whole hero — and the children only shrink to their content because `align-items` is not `stretch`, so changing that silently returns the full section width; **there are two mask geometries as of 2026-08-20 and one shader path** (below 1024px a box around the type, at and above it a half-plane at the type column's right edge, expressed as a box running off the canvas's other three sides so the shader keeps one branch); **the falloff's gap is computed on the CPU** (`uSafeGap`) because the shader's old "distance to the nearer canvas edge" derivation collapses for an off-centre box and turns the feather into a hard edge on the roomy side; **`WAKE_OCCUPANCY_GAIN` must stay below `OCCUPANCY`** or every cell the wake touches clears the threshold and the disturbance renders as a solid block pasted over the ground; **the field's density MUST be measured at more than one viewport** — the safe box tracks the width-capped type, so a bigger window adds field rather than clearing more of it, and the same build measured 10.4% of cells occupied at 1440×900 against 35.4% at 1920×1200. That is why `SAFE_FEATHER` is a *fraction* of the box-to-canvas gap rather than pixels, and why `MIN_SAFE_FRAC_X/Y` floor the cleared zone at a fraction of the canvas. Shipped: 8.4% / 11.3%. Re-measure both viewports when touching any density constant — **on the 2026-08-20 basis, which is inked cells as a fraction of cells outside the type's box** (the old whole-canvas figure stopped being comparable when the field moved to one side, and it always counted the headline's own ink): the centred hero measured 16.3%/16.2%, the split measures 15.0%/19.4%, and a briefly-shipped shorter column feather (0.34) measured 20.8%/24.1% and read as a wall — `SAFE_FEATHER` is **one fraction of 0.62 for both modes**; the section's top/bottom edges get their own fade or the densest band lands under the masthead and reinstates the rejected `--nav-edge` strip; nothing renders before `document.fonts.ready` or the atlas rasterises in the system monospace; the alphabet is hex and **never words** — legible copy here is an assertion nobody wrote. **Don't add Three.js/R3F**; the reference pays ~1.1MB for what two triangles and four render targets do here. The field stands on the Honest-Network Rule's *second* branch only, recorded honestly in §4, and is not a licence for a **figure** made of noise.

- **The headline sets itself one glyph at a time** (`.hero-glyph` + `hero-headline.tsx`, user-directed 2026-08-20, replacing both `.hero-wipe` and the hero's focus pull). No JavaScript and no client component — a server component emits a `--gi` index per character and the stylesheet does the rest. **Four things will look like mistakes and are not.** (1) **Every glyph is a plain inline `<span>`, never an `inline-block`.** Measured at 144px against the shipped GT-Standard Semibold subset: `OPEN SOURCE` sets 1003.250px plain, 1003.313px as inline spans, **1004.047px as inline-blocks**. Chrome shapes the whole inline formatting context as one run and splits at box boundaries, so kerning survives an inline span; an inline-block rounds every advance independently and also flattens badly in the accessibility tree. That is why a per-glyph *transform* or *mask* is closed off — both would need the inline-block. (2) It fills **`backwards`, not `both`** — an entrance ends on the *absence* of what it animated, and `both` would leave thirty permanent stacking contexts under the largest type on the site (verified settled: 0 of 26 glyphs hold a `clip-path`, a `filter` or a `will-change`). (3) **The uncover is a gradient MASK, not a `clip-path`, and every mask declaration lives inside the keyframes** — declared on `.hero-glyph` itself, `mask-image` would survive the animation and `backwards` could not save it. The geometry is arithmetic and easy to get wrong (the first attempt was, leaving the glyph 62% visible at the start): **both the solid and the transparent ends must each be at least one glyph wide**, hence `mask-size: 250%` with `#000 0 40%, transparent 60%` — solid 100% of a glyph, a ramp across 50% of one, transparent 100% of one. Verified: `mask-position: 100%` gives ink mass 0, `0%` is identical to unmasked. There are no vertical insets any more and that is not an oversight — a horizontal mask has no vertical extent to get wrong, which is the whole reason `.hero-wipe` needed `-25%`. (4) **The timing is arithmetic, not taste**: `120 + 29*26 + 226 = 1100ms`, landing exactly with the hairline. The 26ms step is the floor that keeps this a typewriter (under ~25ms adjacent glyphs start on the same frame and it collapses into one sweep); the 226ms duration on **`--ease-out-sine`** is the softness. **The measure that matters is glyphs visibly mid-wash, not nominal in-flight** — the first pass shipped 30ms/110ms on quart and averaged **1.2**, which is one glyph at a time and is exactly what the user then called robotic; 26/226 on sine averages **3.7**. Lengthening the duration alone does not fix it, because quart is ~70% resolved in its first fifth and spends the extra time already finished. **The measured cost is legibility, not LCP**: 620/624/624ms (better than the 648/656/664ms baseline), CLS 0, but **the LCP element moved from `H1` to `P`** — a fully masked headline is not an LCP candidate, so the deck takes over at the same millisecond, and the headline is readable at parse+1100ms instead of parse+740ms. That is what a typewriter is.
