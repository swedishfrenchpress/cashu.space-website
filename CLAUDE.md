## Design Context

This project has a documented design system. Before generating UI, styling components, or making visual choices, read:

- **[PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md)** — strategic spec: register (`brand`), users, brand personality, anti-references, design principles.
- **[DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md)** — visual spec: tokens, type scale, components, named rules, do's and don'ts. Follows the [Stitch DESIGN.md format](https://stitch.withgoogle.com/docs/design-md/format/).
- **[.impeccable/design.json](.impeccable/design.json)** — machine-readable sidecar with tonal ramps, motion, breakpoints, and component HTML/CSS snippets.

**North Star:** *"The Open Specification"* — cashu.space dresses an open protocol in the visual register of a published RFC. Monochrome by doctrine, GT-Standard carries the page, Geist Pixel Square reserved for protocol notation (amounts, mint ids, version strings).

**Hard rules (see DESIGN.md for the full set):**

- **The site is light only** (the One-Scheme Rule, DESIGN.md §2, user-directed 2026-08-17). No dark mode, no `prefers-color-scheme` branch, no `data-theme` override, no toggle, no saved preference; `color-scheme: light` on `:root` keeps the browser's own controls light too. **That is not the same as having no dark surfaces** — the Twilight Stack, the protocol-parts column, the masthead and the Spec pane are still dark plates, and the implementations Card is still a light one. They are *fixed-value surfaces*, dark by composition on a light page. Don't re-add a scheme branch to any of them: each used to carry a second dark value only because a value picked against `#ffffff` contrasts with nothing at `#0a0a0b`, and with one ground there is one value. Where a token still indirects (`--nav-plate` → `--paper`) that is deliberate — the plate is the page punched through the bar, and a literal would keep the pixel and lose the reason.
- Greys shift by lightness, never by hue. Colour exists in exactly two places, both named in DESIGN.md §2: third-party marks (Depicted-World Exception) and the signal green (Signal-Green Exception, user-directed 2026-08-16). The green has **exactly one licensed consumer** as of 2026-08-18: the 7px square opening the wallet-directory status tag. It is never type, a fill, a glow, a gradient, a hover, a focus ring, or a success/error state. **One consumer, so one token** — `--signal-page` (`#1b9d55`). The second, `--signal` (`#6fe3a4`, the mint picked against the always-dark protocol-parts column), was deleted with the property lists that used it rather than left in `:root` unused, which is how a bounded exception widens by accident. The `-page` suffix stays because the two were never interchangeable: `#6fe3a4` measures 1.59:1 on Paper and all but vanishes there, so a future fixed-dark consumer needs a value measured against *that* ground, not the deleted mint reinstated. **Scale is the whole exception**: its largest instance anywhere is 7 pixels square. It was briefly given the hero's pointer plume on 2026-08-16 and taken back the same day — at hero scale a borrowed mint accent read as the pastel/Web3 register PRODUCT.md rules out, and `--signal` itself came from aspensearch.com. If a second consumer is ever proposed again, weigh it by area first.
- No `box-shadow` anywhere — buttons included; they are flat cipher slabs. There is **no exception**: the navbar's condensed box was the one licence and it was retired 2026-08-16 (DESIGN.md §4–5).
- **No `border-radius` in the site's own voice either.** The wallet directory's `Beta` badge was the last rounded surface — a grey `999px` capsule, the Soft-Capsule Exception of 2026-08-14 — and on 2026-08-16 the user directed it into the signal-square family instead: no fill, no border, no radius, a 7px `--signal-page` square then the word in 12px Geist Mono uppercase tracked 0.06em (DESIGN.md §5 Status Tag). Don't put the capsule back, and note the old comment's argument is now *for* this version, not against it — the SaaS-chip tell was "outline + caps + tracking", and there is no container here at all. Keep `STATUS_FACTS` small: the square distinguishes the maturity fact only while it's the one fact carrying one.
- No gradient text, no side-stripe borders, and **no glass anywhere** — translucency has no sanctioned surface left on the site since the navbar's condensed box went, and neither does blur *as a surface*. **Blur is admissible in exactly one form and it is a verb, not a surface** (the Focus-Pull Rule, DESIGN.md §4, user-directed 2026-08-19): type may arrive fractionally out of focus and resolve. Nothing may rest out of focus. It is `.reveal--focus`, opt-in, and it consumes `--reveal-blur`. **The three terms that keep this from being the glass again are all in the focus-pull block in `globals.css` and all of them were measured, not asserted:** it ends on `filter: none` and never on `blur(0px)` (a settled `blur(0)` keeps a compositor layer and grayscale antialiasing on a 9rem headline for the life of the page — verified 0 of 28 entrance elements left holding a filter, an opacity, a transform, or a `will-change` after a full read pass); the blur clears at 55% of the settle, so the rise lands under an already-sharp word and `hero-cipher.tsx` gets a sharp surface for the tail of its pass; and it is never the default, because the masthead sits in a Reveal and a live canvas must not be wrapped in a filter. Radius is per consumer, never global: 18px is a hair under a 9rem headline and a smear under a 13px label. LCP was measured either way on a throttled phone and is unchanged (612/632ms with, 636/624ms without, same LCP element, CLS 0) — **blur does not disqualify an LCP candidate the way `opacity: 0` does, so the 0.02 floor is still the thing carrying that.** The recorded objection stands in §4 and is about register, not cost: a blur-in on display type is the most copied entrance on the web, the rest of this site's motion vocabulary is hard-edged and geometric, and the alternative offered and declined was a per-line mask rise off the `.hero-spec__line` blocks the headline already has. If it is ever reconsidered, start there.
- **Below the fold a section arrives as one gesture, not as a set of adjacent elements** (the Section-Gesture Rule, DESIGN.md §4, user-directed 2026-08-19). One `RevealGroup` (`reveal-group.tsx`) is observed per section — or per row where the section is a list — and its `.reveal-item` descendants are plain server-rendered elements that settle off that one class. No client component, no effect, no observer of their own. The homepage went from twenty observers and ten client components to seven and five. **Granularity is a judgement and both extremes are wrong**: per section would reveal `protocol-parts` entry four while it is a screen and a half below the fold, per element is what this replaced; the unit is whatever the layout already reads as one. **The stagger lives in `globals.css` as one table** keyed on existing class names, not as `delay={}` props across four components. The rise is 20px here against the arrival's 8px, because 8px under editorial type at this scale is a twitch and transform-only movement costs no CLS. **Arrival entrances stay `<Reveal immediate>` and are not groups** — they run from a CSS animation at parse time and a group is gated on an observer, therefore on hydration. **Groups deliberately carry neither `.reveal` nor `.is-revealed`-on-a-`.reveal`**, because `layout.tsx`'s 1.5s failsafe probes for exactly that and groups are among the things it rescues; verified the probe still finds four on the homepage, and that dropping `html.js` leaves all 28 entrance elements visible with `transition-duration: 0s`.
- **The navbar is a full-bleed masthead** (`site-header.tsx`, `nav-clock.tsx`, DESIGN.md §5 Navigation), rebuilt 2026-08-16 on the user's direction against a layout reference. Ink ground, identical in every scroll state: a brand plate hard against the left edge, then one uninterrupted ink run to the right edge carrying a `HH:MM UTC` Geist Mono clock, the links, and the marks. **The plate is the page punched through the bar** — `--nav-plate` is `--paper` and its text is `--ink`; a hardcoded `#ffffff` there is the version the user rejected, and the indirection stays even though the site is light-only and both sides now resolve to one value. **Link order is Wallets, Protocol, Implementations, Spec** (user-directed 2026-08-17): internal routes group in page order and the one outbound link sits last, next to the GitHub mark. Hovering a link (or the current route at rest) drives that same plate across it with `clip-path` over 460ms — the label is rendered twice so the wipe edge passes through the letterforms. The clock updates on the minute, never per second — a per-second field is the animated ticker the anti-references rule out.
- Three things the navbar tried and lost, all user-directed 2026-08-16, none of which should come back: the **Onyx two-state condense** (with `--nav-inset`, `--nav-condensed-max`, `--nav-shadow`, the `--glass-*` ramp, the glass, the radius and the shadow), the **slot** (an open column mid-bar; it read as a hole punched in the bar, worst over the hero), and the **dither strip** (a pixel checkerboard along the bottom edge; it read as noise under the bar). The bar meets the page on a clean line and the value step does the separating.
- Three typefaces only — GT-Standard (everything readable), Geist Mono (technical), Geist Pixel Square (protocol notation). No third.
- Hero headline must be Display: GT-Standard 600, ≥3.75rem, line-height 0.95.
- **The hero sets once and then holds, and its only other motion is the one the reader causes** (the Set-Once Rule and its amendments, DESIGN.md §4). The arrival is: the closing hairline draws across the fold (`hero-rule-draw` in `globals.css`, `--dur-draw-on`), the word *ecash* resolves out of hex (`hero-cipher.tsx`, 760ms, decrypt-only), and the copy settles on the staged `Reveal`, resolving out of a short blur as it does (the Focus-Pull Rule, below). By ~1.1s nothing moves. **No ambient loop, no idle cycle, no timer, no parallax** — don't reintroduce one by increments.
- **The arrival runs from CSS, and two pieces of it will look like mistakes** (Set-Once Rule, second amendment, 2026-08-17). Entrances that always play are `.reveal--arrival`, a keyframe animation that starts when the stylesheet parses; they used to be transitions gated on `.is-revealed`, which meant the whole document — masthead included — was `opacity: 0` blank paper until React hydrated (measured on a throttled phone: legible at 2551ms, LCP 2280ms, against a document complete at 613ms; after: 1178ms and 648ms). **The animation starts at `opacity: 0.02`, not 0, and that is load-bearing**: Chrome won't take an element as an LCP candidate if its only main-thread paint is fully transparent, and a composited opacity animation never repaints on the main thread, so from 0 the whole change buys nothing (measured: LCP 2244ms). **React still adds `.is-revealed` and must keep doing so** — it is what `layout.tsx`'s 1.5s failsafe probes for. `hero-cipher.tsx` now joins that animation's clock or skips its pass entirely, because a word that scrambles after the hero has gone quiet is the ambient motion the rule forbids. The general form: **an entrance that plays unconditionally belongs in the stylesheet; JavaScript is for motion that answers something.**
- **The four faces are subset, and no font lives under `public/` any more** (`scripts/subset-fonts.mjs`, DESIGN.md §3, 2026-08-17): 134,904 bytes down to 39,924, with Geist Mono a static `wght=400` instance at 5KB instead of the full variable face at 71KB. **`assets/fonts/` holds the licensed masters** (all 12 GT-Standard trial faces, the full pixel face) and is never served; **`src/fonts/` holds the generated subsets**, which are the only fonts a browser sees, because `next/font/local` copies them into `_next/static/media/` with a content hash. `public/fonts/` served every master at a stable URL nothing linked to and served the subsets a second time — don't put a face back there. **Re-run the script when the rendered character set changes** — a keymap chord label, a NUT id, the hero field's alphabets, or copy outside Latin-1. A missing glyph does not tofu, it falls through the fallback chain to a system font and still looks fine, so verify by rasterising with the chain stripped. The ranges are deliberately wider than today's text; the reasoning per face is in the script's header. `geist` is a devDependency now — nothing in `src/` imports it. The title block is **centred in the section** (`.hero-spec__inner { margin-block: auto }`, asymmetric section padding for an optical lift of ~5%); the closing hairline is the horizon. Read the **Fold-Line Rule's fourth amendment** before touching the hero's vertical composition.
- **The hero's ground is `hero-field.tsx` + `src/lib/hero-field/`** (2026-08-16, user-directed, after aspensearch.com): a frozen field of Geist Mono hex whose occupancy is Perlin noise, with a **monochrome** wake the pointer stirs through it. **Two alphabets:** hex at rest (entropy — what a Cashu secret is), **currency marks inside the wake** (`₿` weighted 6-in-16, then `$ € ¥ ₩ £ ₹ ₽ ¢ ₱ ₴`), set in `--body`, re-encrypting to `--ghost` behind it. Nothing cycles — each cell has one resting digit and one denomination, both hashes of its coordinates; churning was tried and reads as a slot machine. Hand-rolled WebGL2, ~15KB gzipped. **`₿` (U+20BF) and `₩` (U+20A9) are `.notdef` in Geist Mono and are DRAWN** (`glyphs.ts`): `B` plus four rules clear of the letter, `W` crossed by two. `fillText("₿")` silently falls through to a system font — don't 'simplify' the synthesis away, and don't trust `document.fonts.check` (next/font emits no `unicode-range`, so it reports loadedness, not coverage). **It shipped that afternoon as a Bayer dither with a mint-green fluid plume and was rebuilt the same evening, user-directed, for being too close a copy of the reference** — that first version was a port of Aspen's hero down to its solver constants, and with the masthead clock and the split band it made three borrowings from one source in a day. **The gesture survives, the material does not.** Don't reintroduce the halftone or put green back here. **Four properties are the terms on which the Set-Once Rule readmitted a ground at all — breaking any one breaks the rule, it doesn't extend it:** (1) the field never drifts — there is no `time` uniform in `shaders.ts` and there must not be one; (2) there is **no rAF at rest** — the loop starts on `pointermove`, stops ~2.2s after the last one, and `window.__heroFieldFrames` exists so you can prove it; (3) it stops on a frame bit-identical to the resting one; (4) **the solver** is never allocated under `prefers-reduced-motion` or without a fine hover pointer, but the **ground** mounts for everyone — it is a still image, and withholding it would give every phone a different hero. Also: falloffs multiply **occupancy, not alpha** (fading type with alpha greys the letterforms into a smudge — the ASCII field's lesson); the mask fits the **union of `.hero-spec__content`'s children**, not the block, which is full-width and would clear the whole hero; **`WAKE_OCCUPANCY_GAIN` must stay below `OCCUPANCY`** or every cell the wake touches clears the threshold and the disturbance renders as a solid block pasted over the ground; **the field's density MUST be measured at more than one viewport** — the safe box tracks the width-capped type, so a bigger window adds field rather than clearing more of it, and the same build measured 10.4% of cells occupied at 1440×900 against 35.4% at 1920×1200. That is why `SAFE_FEATHER` is a *fraction* of the box-to-canvas gap rather than pixels, and why `MIN_SAFE_FRAC_X/Y` floor the cleared zone at a fraction of the canvas. Shipped: 8.4% / 11.3%. Re-measure both viewports when touching any density constant; the section's top/bottom edges get their own fade or the densest band lands under the masthead and reinstates the rejected `--nav-edge` strip; nothing renders before `document.fonts.ready` or the atlas rasterises in the system monospace; the alphabet is hex and **never words** — legible copy here is an assertion nobody wrote. **Don't add Three.js/R3F**; the reference pays ~1.1MB for what two triangles and four render targets do here. The field stands on the Honest-Network Rule's *second* branch only, recorded honestly in §4, and is not a licence for a **figure** made of noise.
- **What the hero lost, and what may go back.** `hero-orb.tsx` and `src/lib/orb/` are deleted: the wide dot figure was a `thinking-orbs` blob, which is PRODUCT.md's second anti-reference in silhouette, and it depicted nothing. `src/lib/ascii/` and `ascii-field.tsx` went before it (`terrain.ts` and `vault.ts` were pinned by parity fixtures in cashubtc/wallet, which now have no counterpart here — **a coordination item with that repo**). The hero has a **ground** again (the cipher field, above) but still no **figure**, and the distinction is load-bearing: a ground is stock and owes no assertion, a figure occupies attention and does. So the bar for a figure is unmoved — the Honest-Network Rule's **first** branch, depicting real protocol structure, advancing on scroll or arrival rather than running a clock. The strongest candidate is still the **blind-signature round trip**, recoverable at `git show 5e4c0e8^:src/lib/ascii/bdhke.ts`. If you ever need a ₿ anywhere: **GT-Standard's shipped trial faces cover 271 codepoints and have no U+20BF** (nor `$ ¥ €`), and neither does Geist Mono, so `fillText("₿")` silently renders a system font or tofu — it has to be drawn.
- The **cipher pass** is the site's one typographic motion primitive and now lives in `src/lib/cipher.ts`, shared by two consumers: `button-cipher.tsx` (encrypt→decrypt, on hover, 460ms) and `hero-cipher.tsx` (decrypt-only, once, on arrival). **The hazard both guard against is the same and their answers differ**, so don't "unify" them: contrast extensions assign backgrounds by computed value, and a permanently invisible overlay left in the DOM becomes a visible plate. `hero-cipher.tsx` runs once, so it creates its overlay and **removes** it when the pass settles (measured: zero nodes left). `button-cipher.tsx` re-runs on every hover, so it creates the overlay at enhance time and **hides** it between passes — `display: none` except under `.button-ciphering` (`globals.css`), measured 0×0 — removing it only on unmount. Hiding is what closes the hazard there; re-creating a node per pointerenter would not. Both bail entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

`button-cipher.tsx` reads **visible text only** when it builds its scramble source — it skips `.sr-only` and its own overlay. A CTA carrying `<NewTabHint />` would otherwise paint "READ THE SPEC (OPENS IN A NEW TAB)" in hex across a three-word button. Any future screen-reader-only content inside a `.btn-*` depends on that.
- The homepage's middle is one full-bleed 40/60 split band (`protocol-parts.tsx`, DESIGN.md §5 The Split Spec Sheet), added 2026-08-16 on the user's direction. It replaced the four-parts feature scroller, the tap-to-pay video, and the properties bento in one move; the video, `protocol-demo*.tsx`, the `--fig-*` tokens, and the Demo Panel signature are all deleted. **An entry is an index, a title, a body and a plate — it is not a list** (user-directed 2026-08-18). It carried a fifth element until then: four uppercase Mono properties per entry, each opened by a 7px `--signal` square, which is where the deleted bento's cards went. Cutting them took `.protocol-part__mark` and the `--signal` token with it. Nothing was lost, which is why it was cheap — the bodies already state those properties and the aside's lead carries unlinkability, so the lists were the same fact set said twice in two voices. Don't restate a property as a list here again; row two is the plate alone and the empty column beside it is deliberate. It is deliberately outside the page shell — the entry row needs the width — so don't "align" it back to `--page-max`. Each entry's plate is a `thinking-orbs` canvas driven through the library's engine at ~280px, not its `<ThinkingOrb>` component (which ships only 64px and 20px); see `orb-figure.tsx`. **The loop is capped at 30fps and that cap is deliberate** (2026-08-17): at display rate one visible plate was the most expensive thing on the page, 870ms of script per 3s at 4x CPU throttle, and on a 120Hz panel it was drawing a slow dot field 120 times a second. Capping took it to 241ms. **Device pixel ratio is not the lever and is a recorded dead end** — halving the backing store moved script time 870ms to 878ms, i.e. not at all, because the cost is the library's per-frame path work. It is free because the clock is derived from `performance.now()` rather than accumulated, so a plate that skips frames stays in phase with its neighbours. The Honest-Network Rule was amended for these plates: abstract orbs are material and pass freely, but `connecting` is a topology and is pinned to the Mints entry alone, where Lightning makes a mesh true. Don't move it to another entry or add a second instance. (The hero used to run `listening` and no longer runs anything — these four plates are the only `thinking-orbs` figures left on the site.)
- **The homepage hero is centred on purpose. Everything else on the site is left-aligned.** This is the one deliberate departure from the editorial register, added 2026-08-14 on the user's direction — do not "restore" it as a consistency fix, and do not spread centring to the sections beneath it. Two things depend on it and move together if it ever reverts: the absence of optical-left corrections (headline −0.058em, deck −0.095em) and the headline's escape from the page shell. The third — "the figure moves off-axis" — was struck 2026-08-16; there is no figure. See the Centred-Hero Exception in DESIGN.md §4.
